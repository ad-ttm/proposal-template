Informal conversations with people who invest, September 2026. More are under way.

On Thursday you told us you didn't think we'd "got it" yet, because we'd have put a grand in by now. That was a fair challenge.

The people you need to reach haven't got it either. None of them got far enough to weigh up whether to invest. They stopped at the first hurdle: what is this, and why has nobody told me about it?

So that's the real brief: explain it so simply that the first hurdle disappears.

Three barriers, in order.

Every would-be investor has to get past three questions before they'll put money in. They come in this order, and nobody reaches the second until they're past the first.

  1. 1
    "I don't get it."
    Before anything else, they need to understand how it works and what they'd get back, in pounds rather than percentages. As you said, plenty of people who think of themselves as investors don't work in percentages.
  2. 2
    "Why haven't I heard of this?"
    If it's that good, why hasn't their adviser mentioned it, and why isn't it on the platforms they use? Left unanswered, this turns into suspicion. Answered well, it's our strongest hook.
  3. 3
    "Will I get my money back?"
    Nobody in our conversations got this far yet, but they will. With a two-year wait before the first result, trust matters more than risk. As you put it, anyone can type Goldman Sachs on a page. They need to see who holds their money, who regulates it, and what paperwork they'll receive.

Everything below is built to answer these three questions, in that order.

The ladder.

You've always been a test marketer, so we'd build this in steps. Each one is cheaper than the next, and each one proves something before we spend more. We only climb to the next rung once the one below it is working.

Rung 1
The simple explanation

One plain-English description of how the 10:10 Plan works, short enough to say over a coffee. No jargon, no percentages without the pounds next to them, and a straight answer to "why haven't I heard of this?"

We test it on real investors (the same people who told us "I don't get it") until they can explain it back to us. Everything else is built from it, and IDAD only has to clear the core message once.

Answers: barrier 1. Proves: people can understand it without you in the room.
Rung 2
The written sales letter

A long-form page that tells the whole story in writing. It opens with the question our investors asked: why has my adviser never mentioned this? Then it moves through how it works, the track record, and why the UK is only now catching up with France and Germany, where products like this are the norm.

Writing it first is deliberate. It's quick to produce, cheap to change, and IDAD can mark it up line by line. It's where we find out which angle lands before anyone goes near a camera.

Answers: barriers 1 and 2. Proves: which story makes people want to register.
Rung 3
The guide and email course

A free guide to swap for an email address, followed by a short email course that explains the 10:10 Plan a step at a time. This is how we build the retail list Autocalls doesn't have yet.

After the course, the list gets the maturity anniversaries: regular emails and posts showing what a past issue returned when it matured, "if you'd invested on this day three years ago..." They're real results, told on real dates, and the same content works on social.

Answers: barriers 1, 2 and 3, through repetition. Proves: we can build and warm an audience you own.
Rung 4
The video presentation

You on camera, telling the story from the proven letter: your 20 years with these products, how they work, the track record, and a full section on trust (who holds the money, who regulates it, and exactly what investors receive).

It comes last on purpose. By then we'll know which message works, so you're filming something already proven, and won't need to reshoot.

Answers: all three barriers, with your face and track record behind them. Proves: it can scale.
