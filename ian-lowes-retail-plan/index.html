Operation Retail.

Ian, after Thursday we went away and did two things. We spoke to some real investors, and we had a proper think.

Here's where we've got to. None of it is set in stone. It's here for us to pull apart together.

Here's what the people we've spoken to so far said when being shown the 10:10 Plan.

I don't get it.
Never heard of it.
Explain it to me in simple terms.
Why has my FA never shown me this if it's good?

Friends of Ad's who already invest. More conversations are on the way.

None of them got past "what is this?" They never got as far as deciding whether to put money in.

On Thursday you said we hadn't "got it" yet. Fair enough. It turns out the people we need to reach haven't either. So that's job one: make it simple enough that they do.

And that last question is the best thing anyone's said so far. If we can answer "why has nobody told me about this?", we've got our hook.

Here's what we discussed.

  • The adviser route's done. 22 leads from 3,800 mailers, and no registrations. Advisers will find a reason not to use it, because it doesn't fit how they earn.
  • Retail's open. IDAD has its retail permissions, so Autocalls can trade under them while the AR status is stuck with the regulator.
  • New name, lower way in. Autocalls Limited, trading as Autocalls.com. From around £1,000, with current issues running two years.
  • Traffic first. More visitors means you can bring more products on, and more products means revenue.
  • The big objections. "Why haven't I heard of this?" And with a two-year wait, "will I actually get my money back?"
  • Angles we kicked around. Something the wealthy and IFAs have used for 20 years, now open to everyone. You as the person who's fought to get it out there. The UK finally catching up with France and Germany, where this is normal.
  • Ideas on the table. Fish's long-form video presentation, and Ad's "maturity anniversaries": if you'd invested on this day three years ago, here's what you'd have got back.
You asked us for "some concept or some proposition that's worth testing." So here it is.

Here's what we think would be worth thinking about.

We'd do this in steps, cheapest first, and only move on when the one before is working. You've always been a tester, so this should feel familiar.

  1. 1

    Nail the simple explanation

    One plain-English version of how the 10:10 Plan works. Short enough to say over a coffee, pounds not percentages, and a straight answer to "why haven't I heard of this?" We test it on the same people who said "I don't get it" until it clicks. IDAD only has to clear the core message once, and everything else gets built from it.

    How we'll know: people can explain it back to us.

  2. 2

    Write it up as a sales letter

    A long-form page that opens with "why has my adviser never mentioned this?" and walks through how it works, the track record, and why the UK is only now catching up. Writing comes before video because it's quick, cheap to change, and IDAD can mark it up line by line.

    How we'll know: how far people read, and how many register.

  3. 3

    Give away a guide, then teach by email

    A free guide in exchange for an email address, then a short email course that explains it a bit at a time. That builds the retail list Autocalls doesn't have yet. After that, the list gets the maturity anniversaries: real results, on real dates, and they work on social too.

    How we'll know: sign-ups, opens and clicks, and registrations from the list.

  4. 4

    Then film it

    You on camera, telling the version we already know works: your 20 years with these products, how they work, and a proper section on trust (who holds the money, who regulates it, and what paperwork people get). By then you're filming something proven, so no reshoots.

    How we'll know: how much people watch, and how many go on to invest.

Making the video land with retail investors

The video can't just walk people through the product. It needs a story that stops someone in their tracks, the way the old American investment letters did. Like Fish said on Thursday, this is Britain's best kept investment secret, and that's the story. Two letters we keep coming back to:

Agora Financial, the "Apollo" letter. See it here
"When mankind landed on the moon in 1969, we unlocked the key to a $3.7 trillion bonanza."

It hangs an investment on a moment everyone knows, then promises the hidden story behind it. You can't not read on.

Agora Financial, "Dangerous Lies". See it here
"The 3 Most Dangerous Retirement Lies Brokers Are Telling Americans Today"

It names who's been keeping people in the dark, and puts the writer on the reader's side. That's exactly the question our investors asked: why has my FA never shown me this?

Here's the kind of thing that could look like for the 10:10 Plan:

  • Britain's best kept investment secret
  • The investment the wealthy have used for 20 years, and why your adviser has probably never mentioned it
  • Why investors in France and Germany have done this for years, and the UK is only now catching up

These are starting points to test, not final lines. We'd keep the storytelling and lose the American hype: no promised returns, and everything cleared by IDAD before it goes anywhere.
